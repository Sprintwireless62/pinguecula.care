Benign · very common · caused by UV

That yellow spot on your eye, explained plainly

A pinguecula (say pin-GWEK-you-la) is a small, harmless bump on the white of the eye. It's the eye's version of sun damage — and in sunny Victoria it's something many adults will develop, especially people who spend long hours outdoors. This site explains what it is, how to keep it comfortable, and exactly when to get it checked.

Why this site exists

Our aim is to help you recognise a pinguecula, understand why it forms, and feel confident about what to do next — from simple self-care to knowing the warning signs that mean you should be seen promptly. Everything here is written in everyday language, backed by clinical guidelines and research, and points you to the right health professional at the right time. If a word is unfamiliar, we explain it the first time we use it.

Seek care promptly

When a spot on your eye needs urgent attention

A pinguecula itself is harmless. But some symptoms are not a pinguecula and shouldn't wait. Contact an optometrist, your GP, or an emergency department the same day if you have:

  • • Sudden loss or blurring of vision
  • • Significant eye pain, not just mild grittiness
  • • A spot that grows quickly, bleeds, or changes colour
  • • A growth spreading onto the clear central part of the eye
  • • New light sensitivity, discharge, or a red painful eye
  • • An eye injury or chemical splash

In an emergency call 000. For urgent eye concerns in Victoria, the Royal Victorian Eye and Ear Hospital has a 24-hour emergency department.

How common is it?

~70%

of older adults in the Australian Blue Mountains Eye Study had a pinguecula[6]

Both eyes

are usually affected; most people have at least one by age 70[2]

Outdoor work

is the strongest everyday risk factor for developing one[1,8]

Pinguecula becomes more common with age because it reflects a lifetime of sun exposure. It's especially frequent in people who work or play outdoors — farmers, tradespeople, gardeners, fishers, surfers and lifeguards — and in hot, dry, dusty or windy conditions like much of Australia.[1,3,4]

Reassuring to know

A pinguecula is not cancer and does not spread inside the eye. For most people it never affects sight and needs no treatment beyond sun protection and, if the eye feels dry, some lubricating drops.[1,3,5] Knowing what it is often removes the worry.
