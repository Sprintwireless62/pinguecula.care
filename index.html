That yellow spot in your eye, explained clearly

A pinguecula is a small, harmless bump on formed on top of the white part of the eye. It is a sign of long-term sun exposure, and in a sunny climate like Victoria's, many adults develop one, particularly people who spend a significant amount of time outdoors.[1,2,6]

Why this site exists

This site is written by Tasha Tahir, a Deakin University optometry student, to help you recognise a pinguecula, understand why it forms and feel confident about what should you do next. It covers simple self-care tips, the warning signs to look out for and the health professionals who can help.

Seek care promptly

When a spot on your eye needs urgent attention

A pinguecula itself is harmless. The symptoms below are different and should not wait, because each can point to a more serious eye problem. Contact an optometrist, your GP, or an eye emergency department the same day if you have:

  • • Sudden loss or blurring of vision[16]
  • • Significant or worsening eye pain, rather than mild grittiness[15]
  • • A spot that grows quickly, bleeds, or changes colour[17]
  • • A growth spreading onto the clear cornea at the centre of the eye[13]
  • • New light sensitivity, discharge, or a red and painful eye[14]
  • • Any eye injury or a chemical splash to the eye[16]

In an emergency call 000. For urgent eye problems in Victoria, the Royal Victorian Eye and Ear Hospital has a 24-hour emergency department.[16]

How common is it?

48%

of adults aged 40 and over had a pinguecula in a large general-population study[8]

~89%

of people who have one have it in both eyes[8]

2×

higher odds of having one with regular outdoor activity[8]

Pinguecula becomes more common with age because it reflects a lifetime of sun exposure. It is more frequent in people who work or play outdoors, such as farmers, tradespeople, gardeners, fishers and surfers, and in hot, dry, dusty or windy conditions like much of Australia.[1,7,8]

Reassuring to know

A pinguecula is not cancer and does not spread inside the eye. For most people it never affects sight and needs no treatment beyond sun protection. [1,2,13]
