A pinguecula is a harmless, raised patch that forms on the white of the eye after years of sun, wind, and dust. Most people never need treatment for it — but it helps to know what you're looking at.

Main cause

UV exposure

Three things worth knowing

It's not dangerous

A pinguecula is a benign buildup of protein, fat, and calcium in the eye's clear surface layer. It doesn't turn into cancer and rarely affects vision.

Outdoor life is the trigger

Years of UV light, wind, and dry air — think surfers, farmers, and anyone squinting outdoors — are the usual reasons one forms.

Prevention is simple

Wraparound sunglasses and a wide-brimmed hat cut UV exposure dramatically and are the single best way to slow one down.

Where it sits, and why it shows up on the sun-facing side first

Most pingueculae appear on the side of the eye closest to the nose, since that's where sunlight reflects most directly off the bridge of the nose. Fishermen, farmers, welders, and lifeguards see it often — anyone who spends long hours in bright, dry, or windy conditions.
